The registry principle that holds across the corpus is boundary preservation: each closed system describes a different object and cannot be silently collapsed into another's units — FACS covers visible facial actions, Laban covers qualitative movement organization, skeletal kinematics covers geometry, dynamics covers force [S1][S5]. Every entry must therefore carry its own semantic vocabulary rather than being mapped onto a shared numeric scale, and FACS specifically must stay "a descriptive facial-movement vocabulary, not an emotion detector" [S17]. The second principle is versioned authority: the allowed values are fixed by an external standard (the Paul Ekman Group for AU ids, LMA convention for effort poles), so the repo stores identifiers by group instead of inventing a universal flat cardinality [S13][S18].
